# v0.3.0 § H3 — Focus mode

**Status:** spec, awaiting the owner's markup of `focus-taxonomy.xlsx`.
**Supersedes:** § H2 (`days-per-bonus`) in `docs/designs/v0.2.0-scope.md`.
**Date:** 2026-08-04

## What this is

A fourth panel view. The player picks what they are trying to get out of
education — a working stat, a battle stat, weapon damage, a specific unlock —
and the queue on the schedule view re-sorts so the courses that deliver it come
first.

## Why it exists, and what it kills

§ H2 blocked v0.2.0's fourth ordering mode on one question: what is a "bonus"?
The payload offers two incompatible answers. `workingStatsGain` is machine-
readable but sums three non-interchangeable stats. `learningOutcomes` is what
players actually care about but is free-form English with no common scale.

The measurement that settles it, taken against
`tests/fixtures/education-init-data.json`:

| | `workingStatsGain` | `learningOutcomes` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Courses covered | 131 of 131 | 100 of 131 |
| Lines | 294 | 101 |
| Parse rate | 100% | n/a — free text |
| Distinct strings | 3 stat names | **88 of 101** |

**§ H2's stated objection is wrong and this spec corrects it at source.** H2
claims a summed working-stat sort would be "dominated by whichever stat happens
to use bigger numbers," citing intelligence at 56% of all points. Measured, the
sum ranks courses in a way that correlates 0.558 with an intelligence-only
ranking and 0.557 with an endurance-only ranking — near-identical, so no stat
dominates. The real defect is subtler and worse: the sum is a blend that
matches no player's goal, and only 12 of its top 20 survive in an
intelligence-focused list. A player optimising one stat gets 8 wrong
recommendations with nothing on screen to explain why.

Focus mode dissolves the question rather than answering it. The script stops
deciding what a bonus is worth. The player declares it, and every number on
screen is then true of the thing they picked.

## The taxonomy

Ten categories. Nine come from `learningOutcomes`, classified by Codex and
verified row-by-row against the fixture — 0 invented rows, 0 uncovered outcomes,
101 outcomes expanding to 120 selection rows across **79 selections** and 100
courses. The tenth is `workingStatsGain`, which needs no classification.

**A selection is keyed by `(category, selection)`, never by name alone.** There
are 79 selections but only 75 distinct names: `Strength`, `Speed`, `Defense` and
`Dexterity` each appear under *both* Passive Stat Bonus and Gym Gain Bonus. A
name-keyed registry would merge a passive strength bonus with a strength gym-gain
bonus — precisely the merge the taxonomy forbids, arriving through the back door
as an implementation detail. The compound key is load-bearing, not tidiness.

`focus-taxonomy.xlsx` is the owner's working copy. **The approved sheet is the
input to implementation** — this spec does not restate the 120 rows, and the
build reads them from the approved file, not from this document.

### Three rules the taxonomy already encodes

**Combos are split.** `Gain a 2% passive bonus to speed and strength` is two
rows — selection `Speed` and selection `Strength` — both pointing at SPT2490.
Picking either focus must reach that one course. 19 of the 120 rows are splits.

**Passive and gym bonuses never merge.** `1% passive bonus to strength` and
`1% bonus to strength gains in the gym` are different quantities. Summing them
would be the same category error § H2 blocked.

**Unlocks carry no magnitude.** 22 outcomes have no number at all. They get a
*count*, never a total: "Jail perks: 3 of 4 remaining."

## Architecture

### The focus registry — static map, checked against the payload

The taxonomy ships as a static table keyed by course id, because the
classification is a human judgement that cannot be re-derived at runtime from
88 one-off English strings.

A static map goes stale silently when a third-party catalogue changes, which
is the failure mode `CLAUDE.md` constraint 2 exists to prevent. So each entry
records **the outcome string it was classified from**, and the loader compares
that against the live payload:

- String matches → the classification stands.
- String differs, or the course id is absent from the payload → that entry is
  dropped from the registry and counted.
- A course in the payload carries `learningOutcomes` the registry does not know
  → counted as unmapped.

Both counts surface in the focus view and in the debug report. A stale registry
becomes a visible number, not a wrong answer. This is the same trade the panel
makes everywhere else: `bachelorPrefix` withheld when ambiguous, the finish date
withheld when the plan is unfollowable, `cost unknown` rather than `$0`.

**31 of 131 courses have no `learningOutcomes` at all.** They are not a defect
and must not be reported as unmapped. They are reachable only through Working
Stats, and the view says so in words.

### Data flow

```text
educationInitData ─┬─> parsePayload ──> courses
                   └─> focusRegistry(courses) ──> {entries, stale, unmapped}
                                    │
        settings.focuses ───────────┼──> focusScores(courseId) -> Map
                                    │
                                    └──> orderQueue(queue, mode, courses, scores)
                                                   │
                                          buildPanelModel ──> renderFocusView
```

### Ordering integration

`orderQueue` ([line 440](../../torn-education-scheduler.user.js#L440)) is
already a Kahn topological sort with a per-mode `rank` tiebreak, so
prerequisites-before-dependents is guaranteed by construction and needs no
work. A focus is a rank function and nothing else:

- Add `focus` to `ORDER_MODE_LABELS`, which `normaliseSettings` already
  validates against.
- `rank` for that mode is `-(focusScore(courseId) / durationDays)` — highest
  benefit per day first, matching `shortest-first`'s existing sign convention.
- A course with no score for the active focus ranks last, not zero-and-tied:
  ties fall through to the player's own ordering, which is the existing
  documented behaviour of the tiebreak.

**Ordering still never changes the finish date.** Every honesty note the
settings view already carries applies unchanged, and the focus view repeats it
rather than assuming the player read it there.

### Multiple focuses — lexicographic, never summed

One focus per category, maximum. Ranking is lexicographic: the primary focus
sorts, the second breaks ties, the third breaks what remains. Scores from
different categories are **never added**.

This is the whole discipline of the feature. The moment 1% speed is added to
50 intelligence, the script has invented the exchange rate the design exists to
avoid, and § H2's defect is back with more steps.

### Dedupe by course id

A split course appears once per selection it feeds. Ranking and every
"remaining" total must reduce by course id first. SPT2490 satisfies both Speed
and Strength; a plan containing it must count it once, spend its days once, and
show it once.

### "Remaining out of total possible"

Per selection: the total across the catalogue, minus what the player's
completed courses already granted.

- **Working Stats** — exact. Catalogue totals are intelligence 8,560,
  endurance 3,695, manual labor 3,015.
- **Percentage selections** — summed within a selection only. `Weapon damage:
  12% of 18% remaining` is meaningful; a total across selections is not, and is
  never shown.
- **Unlock selections** — a count, never a magnitude.
- **Selections whose total cannot be stated** — shown as a count with the
  reason. Codex flagged three: weapon experience has no fixed total, rig
  overclocking values are successive limits rather than additive bonuses, and
  the "further" medical and gym bonuses may carry cumulative semantics the
  magnitude alone does not express.

Completed courses come from `completedIds` — "what is already banked" — not
`plannedCompletions`. This is the third distinct use of "completed" in the
codebase and the spec names it deliberately: `completedIds` answers "can this
start today", `plannedCompletions` answers "is this plan followable", and
focus totals answer "what do I already hold". Collapsing any two is the v0.1.0
bug class this release line exists to keep fixed.

### Panel wiring

- `VIEW_TITLES` gains `focus: 'Focus'`; the nav loop at
  [line 1906](../../torn-education-scheduler.user.js#L1906) gains `'focus'`.
- `renderFocusView(doc, body, model, handlers)` alongside the three existing
  view renderers.
- `settings.focuses` is an array of `{category, selection}`, normalised through
  `normaliseSettings` like every other setting, and persisted under the existing
  `tes:settings` key. It is **not** added to `NUMERIC_SETTING_FIELDS`.
- The focus set travels in the share string, and `decodePlan` validates each
  entry against the registry before committing — an unknown selection refuses
  the whole import, matching existing import behaviour.

## Error handling

| Condition | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| Registry entry's recorded string ≠ payload string | Entry dropped, counted, count shown in view and debug report |
| Course id in registry, absent from payload | Same |
| Course has `learningOutcomes` unknown to registry | Counted as unmapped, shown |
| Course has no `learningOutcomes` | Not a defect; excluded silently, explained in view copy |
| Focus selected, no course in queue provides it | Queue order unchanged, view says so |
| Stored focus references a selection that no longer exists | Dropped by `normaliseSettings`, no throw |
| Payload absent (fetch and fiber both failed) | Focus view renders the registry with totals withheld, like the settings view already does |

## Testing

`node --test`, against the existing fixture. New file `tests/focus.test.js`,
plus additions to `tests/order.test.js` and `tests/settings.test.js`.

Must cover:

1. Every taxonomy row resolves to a course id present in the fixture.
2. A split course is reached by both its selections and counted **once** in a
   queue containing it.
3. Lexicographic multi-focus: a two-focus ranking differs from either single
   focus, and no summed score is ever computed — asserted by giving the two
   focuses magnitudes whose sum would reorder the list, and proving it does not.
4. Ordering under every focus leaves the finish date byte-identical.
5. Working-stat totals equal the measured catalogue figures.
6. A registry entry whose recorded string is mutated is dropped and counted,
   not silently trusted — the mutation test that makes the staleness check real.
7. The 31 outcome-less courses are excluded without being counted as unmapped.
8. A percentage total is never summed across selections.
9. An unlock selection reports a count and never a magnitude.
10. Share round-trip preserves the focus set; an unknown selection refuses the
    import and leaves the stored plan untouched.

## Open decisions

- **Unlocks & Abilities is 35 selections over 23 courses — near one-to-one.**
  As a *ranking* focus that degenerates into the course picker that already
  exists. As a *routing* focus it is genuinely useful: "get me to the sports
  shop fastest" hoists that course's whole prerequisite chain to the front.
  This spec keeps it on the routing reading, which is why it stays a category
  as the owner specified. If the owner disagrees, it becomes a filter on the
  schedule view instead and the category count drops to nine.
- **Registry size.** 120 rows adds roughly 130 lines to a 2,857-line file.
  `docs/code-map.md` is already 79.3K against a 134.5K script and has an open
  trimming action. The registry should land as one contiguous, single-anchor
  block so it costs the map one row rather than 120.
- **Priority column in `focus-taxonomy.xlsx`** is the owner's build order, not
  a runtime concept. Nothing in the script reads it.

## What this does not change

- The security surface: one `@match`, `GM_setValue`/`GM_getValue`, no
  `@connect`. Focus mode adds no network access and no new storage key.
- Zero dependencies, no build step, `node --test` only.
- Every do-not-revert item in the v0.2.0 handoff log.
