# Design: v0.2.0 — planner expansion

Date: 2026-08-04
Status: design, approved for planning
Scope contract: `docs/designs/v0.2.0-scope.md` § "v0.2.0". Where this document
and that one disagree, **the scope doc wins on *what*, this one wins on *how*.**

This design covers workstreams A through G. It does not restate the scope doc's
rationale; it records the structural decisions the scope doc left open, and the
three places where evidence from the fixture changed a decision.

---

## Decisions taken before planning

### Staging

One branch (`feat/v0.2.0-planner-expansion`), one `v0.2.0` tag, one PR, **one
commit per workstream** with `npm test` green at every boundary. The scope doc
sanctions cutting into staged tags; a commit per workstream gives review the
same staging without fragmenting the release, and leaves the option of tagging
at a boundary open if the diff proves oversized.

### G2 moves to the front

The scope doc orders G last as "engineering debt". G2 — the purity test's false
positives on the words *window* and *location* **in comments** — is a
prerequisite for every other engine change in this release, because A1, B2, D2,
E and F all add engine code that wants ordinary English comments. Fixing it
first costs one test-only commit and removes a foot-gun from six workstreams.

This is the one ordering deviation from the scope doc, and it is a
strengthening of the same dependency argument the doc uses.

## Evidence that changed a decision

Three probes against `tests/fixtures/education-init-data.json`.

### 1. A degree is a category, and there is exactly one bachelor in each

12 categories, 131 courses, tier counts `{1: 12, 2: 107, 3: 12}`, and **every
category holds exactly one tier-3 course**. The scope doc's "one box per degree"
and its "twelve boxes" are therefore the same set under either reading. § C3 is
built per category, and each category's box is named by its tier-3 bachelor.

### 2. `days-per-bonus` is not determinate — parked to v0.3.0

Two candidate meanings, and they order the catalogue differently:

- **`workingStatsGain`** — present on 131 of 131 courses, parses at 100% against
  `/^Gain ([\d,.]+) (.+?) upon completion$/`, three distinct stats only
  (endurance, intelligence, manual labor). Fully computable. But summing raw
  points across those three is not meaningful — a course granting 50
  intelligence and one granting 5 endurance are not comparable on that scale.
- **`learningOutcomes`** — present on 100 of 131, and free-form English: "Gain a
  1% damage bonus to all weapons", "Gain a bonus of 10% to medical item
  effectiveness", "Decrease an opponent's stealthiness by 0.5". These are the
  perks players actually choose courses for, and **no parse reduces them to a
  comparable scalar.** 1% damage against 0.5 stealthiness has no exchange rate.

Shipping the wrong one gives the mode a name that lies about what it sorts by.
The other three modes are fully determinate, so **E ships three of four modes**
and `days-per-bonus` becomes a numbered v0.3.0 task carrying both candidates.
Once the meaning is settled it is a one-line tiebreak addition.

### 3. Perk inference boundaries confirmed by enumeration

The scope doc's table (§ B2) checks out against a brute-force enumeration of
merits `0..20 step 2` × principal `{0,10}` × WSU `{0,10}`: totals 0–8 and 32–40
have exactly one decomposition, 10–30 have three or four. `inferPerks` is that
enumeration, not a hand-written table, so the boundaries cannot drift from the
rule.

## Structural changes

Everything below is additive to the existing shape: a pure engine section, a
runtime section, one IIFE, no dependencies.

### The panel gains views

`renderPanel` is 120 lines and renders one view. v0.2.0 adds two more (settings,
degree grid). Rendering all three inline would produce a 400-line function.

`renderPanel(doc, mount, model, handlers)` keeps its signature and becomes a
**shell plus a dispatch**: it owns the panel element, the header, the gear/back
control and the collapse behaviour, then delegates the body to one of
`renderScheduleView`, `renderSettingsView`, `renderGridView` by `model.view`.
Each view function takes `(doc, body, model, handlers)` and returns nothing.

`model.view` is `'schedule' | 'settings' | 'grid'`, defaulting to `'schedule'`.
**It is held in `init()`'s closure, not persisted.** `collapsed` is persisted
because a player who hides the panel wants it hidden next visit; a player who
opened settings once does not want settings every visit.

### Settings are a second storage key

`SETTINGS_KEY = 'tes:settings'`, separate from `tes:plan`. A corrupt settings
blob must not cost the player their queue, and the two have unrelated lifetimes.

`freshSettings()` / `loadSettings()` / `saveSettings()` mirror the existing plan
trio, including `savePlan`'s convention of returning a boolean so the caller can
report a failed write.

`loadSettings()` validates **per field, not per object**: an out-of-range
`bookPrice` falls back to its default while every other field survives. Dropping
the whole object because one field rotted is how a player loses settings they
spent time entering.

Schema, with defaults:

| Field | Default | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `maxCooldownHours` | `24` | § D2 |
| `booksOwned` | `0` | § D2 |
| `bookPrice` | `13500000` | § D2, scope doc |
| `jobPoints` | `0` | § D2 |
| `perks.meritsPercent` | `null` (unset) | § B2 |
| `perks.principal` | `null` | § B2 |
| `perks.wsuBlock` | `null` | § B2 |
| `orderMode` | `'as-listed'` | § E |

`null` means "the player has not said", which is distinct from zero. Inference
(§ B2) prefills only `null` fields, and only where the decomposition is unique.

### Acquisition becomes a chain

`fetchEducationData` is unchanged. A new `acquireEducationData()` calls it, and
on `ok: false` falls through to the fiber path, tagging the winning result with
`source: 'fetch' | 'fiber'`. § B3's report names the source, which is the whole
diagnostic value of having two paths.

The fiber work splits along the purity line:

- **Engine (pure):** `searchForPayload(root, limits)` — a bounded breadth-first
  walk over a plain object graph looking for a node that parses as a payload.
  Bounded by node count and depth, with a `seen` set. This is the part that can
  hang a tab, so it is the part that gets pure tests with cyclic and adversarial
  inputs.
- **Runtime:** `fiberRootsFrom(doc)` — scrapes `__reactFiber$*` / `__reactProps$*`
  keys off DOM nodes and hands the objects to `searchForPayload`.

Building it as a fallback the fetch-failure path falls through to, never as a
parallel source of truth, is the scope doc's requirement and is why
`acquireEducationData` is a chain rather than a race.

### Navigation is an observer installed once

`observeNavigation(doc, win, handlers)` patches `history.pushState` /
`replaceState`, listens for `popstate`, and keeps a `MutationObserver` as a
backstop. All three funnel into one debounced `onRouteChange`.

The bootstrap changes from `if (isEducationPage()) init()` to: install the
observer unconditionally, then `init()` if already on the education page. On a
route change, mount when on the education page and unmounted, unmount otherwise.

**Data is re-fetched on each mount.** The old mount node does not survive Torn's
SPA navigation, and the active course's remaining time moves; caching would
serve a stale finish date, which is the one thing this tool must not do.

`init()` must therefore become re-entrant: no module-level mutable state, and
the observer installed exactly once regardless of how many times `init` runs.

### Books arithmetic is pure and closed-form

From the scope doc, `books = (baseTime + maxCooldown) / 12h`, verified against
its worked example: 197 days = 4728h, +24h = 4752h, ÷12 = **396 books**; 396 × 6h
= 2376h saved, leaving 2352h = **98 days**. Both figures reproduce exactly.

`booksCeiling({ baseSeconds, maxCooldownSeconds })` returns that floor. The
panel reports two dates:

- **Planned** — using `min(booksOwned, ceiling)`.
- **Floor** — using the full ceiling, **with its cost printed beside it**. The
  scope doc is explicit that the date alone misleads: "98 days for 5.35b" is
  actionable, "98 days" is not.

The floor is computed independently, never as the planned date minus leftovers.

**Stated assumption:** book savings apply to *queued* course time, not to the
active course's remaining time. `activeCourse.completedAt` is an anchor read
from Torn and the script cannot know what the player will spend on a course
already running. The panel says so rather than quietly assuming either way.

### Export/import carries no encoder

Base64 would mean `btoa`, which is a host global the engine may not touch and
the test sandbox does not provide. The format is therefore a flat,
version-prefixed, pipe-delimited string:

```
TES1|q=34,57,58|c=24|b=396|p=13500000|j=0|m=20|pr=1|w=1
```

Readable, trivially parsed with string operations, and **impossible to evaluate
as code** — which is the security property the scope doc asks for, achieved
structurally rather than by discipline. `decodePlan` validates every course id
against the live catalogue and rejects unknown ids with a named reason.

### The debug report is a pure builder over an explicit input

`buildDebugReport(input)` is an engine function taking an object whose fields
are exactly the allowlist. It cannot leak what it is never handed — the
allowlist is enforced by the function signature, not by a filtering step inside
it. `gatherDebugContext()` in the runtime constructs that input.

Tests assert the exclusions directly against the rendered string: the `rfcv`
token, any raw payload, the completed-course set, and `activeCourse.completedAt`
must not appear.

The report renders in the panel before it can be copied. A copy button that
hides its payload is how people leak things they did not know they had.

### Placeholder URLs render nothing

`GREASY_FORK_URL` and `FORUM_POST_URL` are `null`. Every consumer guards on
null and renders nothing — not a dead link, not a `#` href. § B3's contact line
still names Greasy Fork in prose, because the instruction is useful without the
URL; only the link element is withheld.

## Testing

Every workstream adds tests in the existing style: `node --test`, no
dependencies, through the `loadUserscript` harness. Each new exported function
is added to `EXPORT_NAMES` **in the same commit that adds the function** —
omitting it is the harness's documented failure mode ("is not a function").

Specific coverage the workstreams must carry:

- `searchForPayload` against cyclic graphs and graphs that exceed both bounds
- `inferPerks` across every total 0–40, asserting the unique/ambiguous split
- `buildDebugReport` exclusions, asserted against the rendered string
- `decodePlan` against truncated, unknown-id, and non-numeric input
- `booksCeiling` against the scope doc's 396/98-day worked example
- `orderQueue` output remaining a valid topological order under every mode —
  reusing `prereq.test.js`'s catalogue-wide property-test shape
- `init()`'s try/catch, via a mount whose first `appendChild` throws (G1)

`schedule()`'s order-independence property test must keep passing unchanged
after § E lands. Ordering changes time-to-benefit, never the total.
